At GEMS-Taiwan, we are part of the iGEM program, an international synthetic biology competition where high school and college students from around the world collaborate to develop innovative synthetic biology solutions to real-world problems. Through our outreach, we introduce students to the fundamentals of synthetic biology in an engaging and accessible way.

Our goal is to foster interest and make awareness of synthetic biology among future generations of scientists. We achieve this through:

  • Organizing informative workshops with schools in an inclusive way
  • Creating educational resources that simplify complex synthetic biology concepts
  • Hosting informative events for the public to raise awareness of synthetic biology
  • Making GEMS-Taiwan the go-to place for learning about synthetic biology in Taiwan

2022

Fusarium won't

In 2022, GEMS-Taiwan worked on a synthetic biology solution to help defend Cavendish banana plants from Fusarium wilt (a fungal disease threatening banana crops worldwide). Their approach focused on engineering a beneficial probiotic bacterium that could protect banana roots, potentially slowing down the spread of the disease.

2023

Cure-All Reef

In 2023, the team's project "Cure-All Reef" aimed to improve coral reef resilience. Their synthetic biology solution involved engineering bacteria to better deliver probiotics to corals, helping protect them from bleaching and disease.

Click Here to explore: https://2023.igem.wiki/gemstaiwan/home
2024

Dengue Beeters

In 2024, the team worked on "Dengue Beeters," a synthetic biology solution to combat dengue fever by disrupting the mosquito life cycle with innovative biological tools and designs. The project included a 3D-printed on-trap and other novel strategies to control mosquito populations, which contributed to their success in the competition and winning the Grand Prize in the High School Division at the 2024 Jamboree.

2025

Textile Fighters

In 2025, GEMS-Taiwan worked on solving the problem of textile and plastic waste by focusing on ways to break down materials that are normally very hard to recycle. The team engineered and tested different enzymes that can digest components of blended fabrics, developed methods to identify and preprocess different types of textiles, and created tools to help classify fabrics more easily.
